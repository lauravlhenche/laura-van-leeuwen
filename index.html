About

Your book in Spanish,
with its voice intact.

Literature has shaped how I read and how I translate. My degree in philology and master's in literature from Complutense University of Madrid taught me to look past the surface of a sentence and see how voice, structure, and word choice create the reader's experience. I bring that same attention to every book, first understanding what the author is doing before deciding how to carry it into Spanish.

When I start a book, I look for what makes the writing distinct: what a character leaves unsaid, how the pace shifts between scenes, which small details resurface later. A pause, a repeated word, an unusually short sentence—these choices usually mean something, and I need to understand them before translating them. I stay close to the author's meaning while shaping each sentence naturally in European Spanish. I track character voices, recurring images, and story details across the whole book, then read the Spanish version on its own terms. It has to work as a book in its own right, not just as a reflection of the English original.
